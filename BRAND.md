# LinkNMS

## Manifesto

A house is the largest sum most people ever spend at once, and it is built on
memory — who agreed to what, what changed, what it costs now. Memory is
exactly what fails when money is on the line, and when it fails the two sides
are left with two versions of the same conversation.

LinkNMS replaces memory with a record. One record, that every side can see.
It shows each change and who made it. And it is honest about the one thing it
cannot do: it reveals that a record was altered, it does not prevent the
alteration — tamper-evident, never tamper-proof.

No side owns it against the other. Everyone argues from it. That is the whole
idea, and everything else is detail.

## Strategy

### Overview

LinkNMS is a collaboration platform for building a house. It connects the
homeowner with every person doing or managing the work — general contractor,
specialty subcontractors, site manager, architect, inspectors — around one
shared, verifiable record of what was agreed, what changed, and what it cost.
Its reason to exist is the moment a build goes sideways: "who decided this,
when, and how much did it move the budget?" LinkNMS turns that from an
argument into a lookup.

Sold as SaaS. The account is purchased by one party — either the homeowner
or the contractor — but paying for the account never means owning the record
against the other: whoever holds the subscription, the platform stays the
neutral ground every party on the build works from.

### Audience

- **Homeowners** running a build (turnkey, direct-to-specialty, or hybrid) who
  are spending a large sum on something they cannot personally supervise
  full-time, and who fear being told after the fact that scope, timeline, or
  price changed.
- **Builders, contractors, subcontractors and site managers** who want a
  defensible record as much as the owner does — proof that a change was
  requested and approved, not invented later.

Both are non-technical. Both have been burned by "he said / she said." Neither
wants a heavyweight enterprise construction suite; they want a clear shared
ledger for one build.

### Positioning

LinkNMS is the shared, verifiable record for a house build — the neutral
ground where every party works from the same facts.

It is explicitly **not**:

- **Not tamper-*proof*.** It is tamper-*evident*: it reveals after-the-fact
  edits, it does not prevent them. These are different properties and LinkNMS
  never blurs them.
- **Not a BIM authoring or analysis suite.** LinkNMS accepts BIM file uploads
  and previews them where the format allows, attaching each model to the shared
  record. It does not author or edit models, run clash detection, or replace
  tools like Revit or ArchiCAD — the model is evidence on the record, not the
  workspace it was made in.
- **Not a municipal-permitting integration.** It stores links and marks
  permitting phases; it does not talk to city systems that, in most markets, do
  not yet expose an API.
- **Not an enterprise ERP.** A builder runs several builds at once, so LinkNMS
  is multi-build by design — switch between them and see them all in one
  portfolio view. What it is *not* is the company's back office: no accounting,
  payroll, procurement, or CRM. It is the shared record for the work, not the
  system that runs the firm.

### Personality

Neutral referee. Precise. Calm. Honest to a fault. LinkNMS behaves like a
well-kept official logbook, not a salesperson — it states what is true,
including what it cannot do, and it never takes one party's side against
another's.

### References & Anti-References

- **The construction site logbook (livro de obra).** Borrow: the authority of
  an official, chronological, trusted record every party defers to. Don't copy:
  paper's opacity — un-searchable, easy to lose, impossible to verify.
- **Version control (Git).** Borrow: an immutable, verifiable history where
  every change is attributed. Don't copy: developer-facing complexity and
  jargon; LinkNMS users are homeowners and builders.
- **Anti-reference: "trustless" crypto pitches.** Rejected outright. LinkNMS
  does not promise mathematical certainty about the real world; the weak link
  is never the record, it's the people using it. We say so plainly.
- **Anti-reference: enterprise construction suites (Procore-scale).** Rejected
  for this audience: their breadth is their barrier. LinkNMS stays narrow and
  legible for the parties on one house.

### Promise

Everyone argues from the record, not from memory.

### Guardrails

- Never say "tamper-proof," "unhackable," or "bulletproof." The only approved
  term for the change history is **tamper-evident**.
- Never claim a feature that isn't built. LinkNMS is currently a product
  definition; nothing in it may be described in the present tense as if it
  ships today.
- Never dress up a limitation. Stating what LinkNMS can't do is on-brand, not
  off-message.
- Never take a side. Copy and product address every party as equals.

Litmus test: *Could this line survive a disappointed builder reading it back
to you six months in?* If it overpromises certainty, security, or a missing
feature, it fails.

## Voice

### Identity

Trust through clarity. LinkNMS speaks like a competent, unhurried professional
who has read the fine print and refuses to oversell. Plain nouns over hype.
The confidence comes from precision and candor, not adjectives — the same
qualities that make a record credible in a dispute.

### Tagline & Slogans

- **Primary:** One record every side trusts.
- The build, on the record.
- Argue from the record, not from memory.

### Message Pillars

1. **One shared record, in the open.** Every party sees the same tasks, the
   same budget, the same history — no private second set of books. That
   transparency is what lets owner, contractor and every specialty collaborate
   from the same facts instead of negotiating from separate ones.
2. **Every change is accountable.** Who changed what, when, and by how much,
   captured in a history that reveals any after-the-fact edit.
3. **Honest about what it proves.** LinkNMS tells you exactly what its record
   guarantees — and what it doesn't.

### Phrases

- "Everything on the record, in the open, for every party."
- "One record every side can see, and collaborate from."
- "Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof."
- "What it is — and what it isn't."
- "The same page, for every side."

### Tonal Rules

- Lead with what's true, limitations included. If a feature doesn't exist yet,
  say so in the same breath.
- Prefer precise nouns to intensifiers: "verifiable change history," not
  "revolutionary security."
- Address every party as equals in the same sentence; never write copy that
  reads as the owner's tool against the builder, or vice versa.
- When describing the change history, state the boundary every time: it
  reveals that a record was altered; it does not prevent alteration.
- No fear-selling. Name the real problem (disputes) soberly; don't dramatize
  it.

### Vocabulary

**Say:**

- *the record*, *the shared record*, *a verifiable record* — the product's
  centre of gravity; prefer it to "the platform" or "the app."
- *tamper-evident*; *reveals an after-the-fact edit* — the only approved way
  to describe the change history.
- *planned vs. actual*; *Owner side / Builder side* — the two-sides framing.
- *Trade Contract* — the scoped agreement for one specialty (its own party,
  scope, budget and timeline). Capitalised; it is a defined concept.
- *build* (a house build), *every party*, *both sides*, *neutral ground*.

**Avoid:**

- *tamper-proof*, *unhackable*, *bulletproof*, *blockchain-secured*,
  *trustless* — all promise prevention or certainty LinkNMS does not offer.
- *immutable* — acceptable only in internal technical notes about the history;
  never in market copy, where it reads as "tamper-proof."
- *revolutionary*, *seamless*, *military-grade*, *next-generation* — hype
  adjectives standing in for proof.
- *users* where *the owner and the builder* (or the specific role) is clearer.

### Social Bios

- **One-liner (≤ 80 chars):** One record every side of a house build trusts.
- **Short (≤ 160 chars):** The shared, verifiable record for a house build —
  neutral ground where the owner and everyone doing the work argue from the
  record, not from memory.
- **Long:** LinkNMS is the shared, verifiable record for building a house. It
  gives the homeowner, the contractor and every specialty one place to see the
  same schedule, budget and scope — and a change history that reveals who
  altered what, and when. Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof, and honest about
  the difference. No side owns it against the other.

## Visual

### Core Colors

Two identity colors carry the brand's central idea — the two sides of a build,
side by side — anchored by a drawing-paper neutral pair.

- **Owner Blue** — `#3e5c8a` — the homeowner's side: what was planned and
  agreed. Mandatory identity color.
- **Builder Orange** — `#b4633b` — the builder's side: what actually happened
  on site. Mandatory identity color.
- **Ink** — `#16181d` — near-black for text and marks, the "graphite" of the
  record. Mandatory.
- **Paper** — `#fbfaf7` — warm off-white, the calm surface the record sits on.
  Mandatory.

These four were re-based in **LINA-86** (from `#2a78d6` / `#eb6834` / `#0b0b0b`
/ `#fcfcfb`) to the desaturated set the design file had already moved to. The
driver was accessibility as much as art direction: the retired Owner Blue
scored 4.30:1 on Paper and so failed WCAG AA for body text despite being a
mandatory identity colour; the current value scores 6.48:1 and passes.

The web app (`app/`) still renders the retired palette by deliberate decision —
its migration is scheduled separately and is not a bug to be fixed in passing.

The Owner Blue / Builder Orange pairing is the signature: planned and actual
are always distinguishable at a glance, never merged into one voice.

### Typefaces

- **Inter** — role: everything (interface, data, and marks), with the system
  stack (`system-ui`, `-apple-system`, `Segoe UI`, `sans-serif`) retained as
  the fallback. Adopted in **LINA-86**; the brand previously specified the bare
  system stack. The intent is unchanged — a neutral, highly legible face keeps
  the record feeling like a document, not a campaign — but Inter makes that
  neutrality consistent across operating systems instead of varying per device.
- **Archivo** — role: display only, on the landing page (LINA-80). It does not
  appear in the product UI.
- Licensing: both faces are SIL Open Font Licence and are self-hosted via
  `next/font`, so no commercial licence and no third-party request at runtime.
  The system fallbacks ship with the target operating systems.

### Art Direction

Engineering-drawing calm. Warm off-white paper, thin precise grid and baseline
lines, generous quiet space, and data as the hero — timeline bars, budget rows,
timestamped changes. The two identity colors appear as marks *on* the record
(a planned bar vs. an actual bar), never as decorative fields. Nothing shiny,
no gradients-as-flourish; the aesthetic of a trustworthy technical document
that happens to be alive and searchable.

### Logo & Marks

The identity is a wordmark plus a small mark, both expressing the two-sides
idea rather than a pictorial symbol.

- **Wordmark** — *LinkNMS*, set in the brand's system sans, medium-to-bold
  weight, exact casing (see Naming). The wordmark is the primary mark and may
  stand alone.
- **The two-sides glyph** — a square split into an Owner Blue half and a
  Builder Orange half (a clean diagonal or vertical division), read as
  "planned and actual, side by side." Used in a lockup to the left of the
  wordmark, or alone as an avatar/app icon.
- **Clear space & legibility** — keep clear space around the lockup equal to
  the height of the "L"; never place it so small that the split in the glyph
  stops reading, and never on a busy photographic background.
- **Monochrome fallback** — Ink on Paper (or reversed, Paper on Ink) when a
  single colour is required; the split then reads as a tonal division.
- **Misuse** — do not recolour the glyph outside the two identity colours,
  do not add shadows, gradients, glows or outlines, do not stretch or rotate
  it, do not letter-space or re-case the wordmark, and do not merge the two
  halves into a single flat colour (that erases the whole idea).
- **Status** — this is the current working identity, not a registered
  trademark; no ™/® claim is approved, and no co-branded or partner lockups
  are approved. Treat exact geometry as design-owned; this section governs
  meaning and misuse, not construction specs.

### Photography & Illustration

- **Illustration leads.** The primary imagery is the record itself —
  timeline bars, budget rows, timestamped changes, the planned/actual split.
  It is product-truth, drawn in the engineering-drawing register, not
  decorative spot art.
- **Photography, if used, is honest.** Real, un-staged construction sites and
  real documents in natural light. No glossy hero shots, no stock clichés
  (smiling clients shaking hands, pristine hard-hats, generic blueprints), no
  imagery that dramatises conflict to sell fear.
- **Never party-biased.** No image should frame the owner as watching over the
  builder, or the builder as the hero of the build; both appear as equals or
  the work appears without either.
- **Accessibility carries into imagery.** Any diagram that uses Owner Blue and
  Builder Orange labels them too — the colourblind commitment is not limited
  to the interface.
- **Status** — no approved photo library or illustration set yet exists; this
  is direction for creating one, not a sign-off on specific assets.

## Governance

### Naming

- **Brand name and domain:** LinkNMS. This is the single name, user-facing and
  internal. No codenames, no second name in the documentation, no third name on
  the repository.
- Written **LinkNMS** — not "Linknms", not "LINKNMS", not "Link NMS."

### Claims

- **Draft candidates (not yet substantiated — do not publish as fact):**
  "tamper-evident change history," and any claim of uptime, reliability,
  customer counts, "used by builders," security certifications, or scale.
  LinkNMS is currently a product definition; every one of these requires
  evidence before it is used in market-facing copy.

### Accessibility Commitments

- Owner Blue and Builder Orange must **never** be the only thing distinguishing
  planned-vs-actual data or one party from another. Always pair the color with
  a label, pattern, or position so the record is legible to colorblind users —
  a dispute-resolution record everyone can read is a non-negotiable, and this
  commitment may only be raised, never relaxed.
