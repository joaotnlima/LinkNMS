# Positioning & Messaging — the Golden-Triangle reframe

**Status:** draft for CEO sign-off (in_review). External messaging cannot ship without it.
**Owner:** Marketing Manager · **Reviewer:** Chief of Staff (CEO)
**Supersedes:** the cost/budget-only anchor. **Triggered by:** board feedback on the roadmap (LINA-12).

---

## 1. What changed, in one line

The mission is unchanged — **argument → lookup**. What widens is *what* we turn
into a lookup. A single decision or change order never moves only cost: it
**redraws scope, ripples the schedule, and can carry a quality or inspection
consequence.** Positioning must make all four visible — **scope, time, cost, with
quality as the binding constraint** — instead of anchoring on budget alone.

The old anchor ("who decided this, when, and how much did it move the *budget*?")
was true but narrow. It sold the product as budget insurance — a rare event
optimists discount. The golden-triangle frame sells the thing the record does on
an ordinary Tuesday: keep the four things that actually move a build **legible to
everyone who is on the hook for them.**

## 2. The golden triangle, in LinkNMS terms

Every construction decision trades against three constraints; quality is the line
you are not allowed to cross while you trade. LinkNMS does not *manage* the
triangle — it makes the **movement** in it a matter of record.

| Pillar | The question it answers | What a change moves | What LinkNMS turns into a lookup |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | *What did we agree is included?* | Adds, deletes, substitutions | The current agreed scope per contract, and the gaps between contracts |
| **Time** | *When, and what does slipping it hit?* | Milestones, dependency chains | The schedule impact of each change, and who it unblocks or stalls |
| **Cost** | *How much did that just move?* | Contract value, cost deltas | The budget delta tied to the decision that caused it |
| **Quality** *(binding constraint)* | *Does it still pass?* | Inspection, spec conformance | Acceptance criteria and sign-offs attached to the work, before the next trade depends on it |

**Why quality is the binding constraint, not a fourth equal pillar.** You can
trade scope for time, time for cost, cost for scope — that is negotiation. You
cannot trade *below* the quality line without it becoming a defect, a failed
inspection, or a dispute later. Framing quality as "the constraint you move the
other three within" is both truer to how a build works and sharper than a flat
list of four. It also protects the neutrality of the record: quality is the one
pillar where "who signed off, against what criteria" prevents the argument
outright.

**The spine still holds.** For every pillar the promise is identical: turn *"who
decided this, when, and what did it move?"* from an argument into a lookup. We
widened the object of the sentence, not the verb.

## 3. Positioning statement

> **For** homeowners building and the pros who build for them,
> **who** make the decisions that move a build verbally, on site, over months —
> and end up with no shared record either side accepts,
> **LinkNMS is** one shared record of the build
> **that** makes every change visible across scope, time, cost and quality — the
> moment it happens, to everyone on the hook for it.
> **Unlike** WhatsApp-plus-goodwill or a spreadsheet one party owns,
> **LinkNMS** is the neutral third thing both sides are in — so "who decided this,
> when, and what did it move?" is a lookup, not an argument.

**One-liner (external):** *One shared record of the build — so every change to
scope, time, cost and quality is a lookup, not an argument.*

**Tagline (unchanged, brand-owned):** **Trust built-in.**

## 4. The competitive frame: against "we just email and spreadsheet it today"

The bar is **not** "better than nothing." It is **better than WhatsApp plus
goodwill**, for people not looking for software. We win by naming the specific
failure of each incumbent — never by claiming to be more powerful project
software (that market exists and is not ours).

| They say | We say |
|---|---|
| "WhatsApp works fine." | WhatsApp records everything and finds nothing. It has no *state* — only a stream. Scroll it for six months and still nobody can say what the current agreed scope is. |
| "I keep a spreadsheet." | It's *your* version. The other side never agreed to it and usually never sees it. A record only one party holds settles no argument. |
| "The site manager has it in his notebook." | Not shared, not searchable, and it belongs to one side. |
| "That's what construction software is for." | Built for companies running many projects, not two parties on one house. Overkill nobody on a single build will adopt. |

**The one thing we never say:** that this protects you from a dishonest
counterparty. The common case is two honest people with two incompatible
memories. Framing LinkNMS as a policing tool guarantees the side carrying the
daily cost refuses to use it — which destroys the only thing that makes the
record worth anything: **both sides are in it.**

## 5. Messaging that lands separately — homeowner vs. trade

Same record, two motives. Never blend them on a single surface.

### 5a. Homeowner — *reassurance across four pillars*

**Core emotional job:** confidence in something they cannot supervise or
technically judge. They respond to signals ("on track / attention / delayed"),
not raw numbers, and they check for reassurance more than data.

**Message spine:** *Know where your build stands — scope, schedule, budget and
quality — without needing to be a builder.*

| Pillar | Homeowner promise |
|---|---|
| Scope | "See exactly what you're paying for on each contract — and where the gaps between them are, before they become someone's surprise." |
| Time | "One timeline across every trade. Know if a two-week slip is normal or a problem — the app tells you which." |
| Cost | "Every change to the budget, the moment it's agreed — not a shock on the final invoice." |
| Quality | "See what's been inspected and signed off before the next trade builds on top of it." |

**Proof-of-reassurance principle:** a quiet week has value. "No changes this week"
is a message worth sending to a first-time owner — reassurance is the product,
not just alerts.

### 5b. Trades (GC, site manager, subcontractor) — *self-protection + getting paid*

**Core motive:** stop being interrupted, and stop arguing for money you believe
you're owed. The pro does **not** want to be watched. They want the change they
agreed in passing to still exist, in writing, when it reaches the invoice.

**Message spine:** *Get paid for exactly what was agreed — and stop
re-litigating it from memory.*

| Pillar | Trade promise |
|---|---|
| Scope | "A precise line on what's in and out of your contract — so 'whose job is this' is a lookup, not a standoff." |
| Time | "The dependency trigger that says when you can actually start — and a record of who held you up when you couldn't." |
| Cost | "Every change order logged when it's agreed, tied to a milestone. Bill it without defending it from memory." |
| Quality | "Written acceptance criteria per task — verified once, so completed work isn't reopened later." |

**Adoption truth we design messaging around:** the cost is paid daily, in small
increments, by the trade; the benefit lands rarely, in large increments, mostly
when something goes wrong. So trade messaging must sell the **daily** win (fewer
interruptions, faster clean billing), not the rare dispute payoff. If we only
sell the dispute, we're selling insurance to someone who's sure they won't need
it.

## 6. Canonical audience map

This brief **adopts the human-documented personas under
`cowork/documentation/personas/` as the canonical audience map** for all
marketing work — superseding any ad-hoc segment list. Marketing groups those
eleven roles into three go-to-market audiences:

1. **Homeowner** (`personas/owner/*`, + Client's Representative) — the buyer and
   champion; reassurance-led. Note the three variants: first-time (needs
   interpretation, green/yellow/red), experienced/investor (wants the numbers),
   shared-decision couple (two approvers — a process question, flagged to PM).
2. **Prime trade** (`personas/contractor/*`, `personas/site-manager/*`) — GC,
   growing/premium/volume contractors, foreman and technical site manager;
   self-protection- and payment-led. The daily user whose adoption makes or
   breaks the record.
3. **Specialist & oversight** (`personas/subcontractor/*`,
   `personas/architect/*`, `personas/quality-inspection/*`, `personas/hse/*`,
   supplier) — narrower scope, addressed later; messaging inherits the "get paid
   / prove it once" spine.

Licensing authority and neighbour are **not users** (no-login, informed-about
personas) and are never marketing targets. This map is the audience source of
truth; segment-specific campaigns reference the persona files by name.

## 7. Voice & guardrails (brand consistency)

- **House Record** mark and **Trust built-in.** tagline on every surface; do not
  alter the mark. Owner Blue = planned/owner side, Builder Orange =
  actual/builder side — always paired with a label or position, never color
  alone.
- **Neutral, never partisan.** The record belongs to both sides. No copy that
  frames one party as the risk to guard against.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the dispute we prevent ("the wall isn't where
  you remember agreeing"), not "collaboration" or "visibility" in the abstract.
- **No overclaiming.** The record shows *if* it was altered; it does not *prevent*
  alteration and is **not legal proof**. Never imply either. This is a
  trust-preserving boundary, not a legal disclaimer.
- **Useful on an ordinary Tuesday**, complete when the argument arrives — lead
  with the daily utility, let the dispute payoff be the closer.

Full funnel instrumentation is a follow-up once the site exists; this brief fixes
the *frame* each surface is measured against.

## 9. What this routes to Product (feedback, not scope commitment)

Two product-scope implications surfaced from this reframe. They are **feedback for
the Product Manager to weigh against R0 cut lines on LINA-12 — not a scope
commitment, and this brief does not edit the roadmap.** Routed via a delegated
issue to the PM:

1. **Four-pillar change order.** For the "argument → lookup" promise to hold
   across all four pillars, a change-order record should be able to express its
   impact on **scope, time, and cost together, with a quality/inspection flag** —
   not cost alone. If R0 ships cost-only, marketing narrows the change-order
   claim accordingly to avoid overpromising.
2. **Homeowner four-pillar dashboard.** The homeowner reassurance message assumes
   a single view that rolls up all four pillars as simple signals
   (on-track/attention/delayed). If R0 ships a subset, the homeowner message
   scopes to whichever pillars are real.

Marketing will not make a public claim ahead of what R0 actually ships; these
items exist so the PM can decide, and so our copy tracks the real product.
