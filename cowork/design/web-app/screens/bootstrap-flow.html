What happens next

The contractor accepts, then uploads the plan of actions and timelines. Your master timeline fills in from there — you'll be notified.
Nothing is tracked yet. The record starts once the plan is imported.
Why the model is asked first (screen 3). The operating model decides who gets invited on the very next screen — one GC (turnkey), each trade (direct), or both (hybrid). It's a property of the build, captured once and never re-prompted. This is the first-time owner's anxiety budget respected: they don't have to "learn to manage a build."
Empty ≠ dead end. Screens 1 and 7 are empty states with a single primary action, not blank dashboards. The stressed, first-time owner is never left guessing what to do next.
Neutral referee. The invited party carries the orange contractor tag from the first moment; the owner is blue. Side is set by position + label + colour, never colour alone — legible in bright site light and for colour-blind users.

Why explicit mapping, not auto-detect. The whole product exists to kill “I thought that was included / assumed it was ready.” Guessing the columns would reintroduce exactly that ambiguity at the worst moment — the foundation of the record. So the GC confirms the meaning of every column, sees a live preview, and per-row problems surface as warnings that don't block (import now, fix in place) while only a missing required mapping blocks Confirm. The Dependency column becomes the dependency-gated start; Trade seeds the Trade Contract. New components mapped in Storybook: EmptyState, Stepper, PartyInvite, FileDropzone, ImportMapping, WBSTree.
