THE MARK

“House Record” — the logo is the mark

A house with its roof split at the ridge into Owner Blue and Builder Orange, over a paper body — the record — holding a longer planned bar (blue) and a shorter actual bar (orange). The two sides of a build, side by side, over planned vs. actual. Drawn in the engineering-drawing register: flat colour, thin ink outline, no gradient or shadow.

The icon is the logo. The wordmark and slogan are added only where a surface needs them — each context decides whether to show the name, and how.

PROTECTION

Clear space & misuse

Keep clear space around the lockup equal to the height of the “L”. Never place the mark so small that the split stops reading, nor on a busy photographic background.

LinkNMS

Do

  • Keep the roof split in the two identity colours.
  • Use the mono fallback (Ink on Paper) when one colour is required.
  • Give the lockup clear space and a calm surface.

Don't

  • Recolour the split, or merge the two halves into one flat colour.
  • Add shadow, gradient, glow or outline; stretch, rotate or re-case the wordmark.
  • Bake the wordmark into the icon asset, or shrink it until the split blurs.

VOICE

Voice, in one breath

Trust through clarity. Plain nouns over hype; lead with what’s true, limitations included. Address every party as equals.

Say

  • “One record every side trusts.”
  • “Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof.”
  • “Planned vs. actual · Owner side / Builder side.”

Avoid

  • “tamper-proof”, “unhackable”, “bulletproof”, “blockchain-secured”.
  • “revolutionary”, “seamless”, “military-grade”, “next-generation”.
  • Copy that frames the owner against the builder, or vice-versa.
