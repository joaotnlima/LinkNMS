# The first-time owner

## Who they are

Building or renovating for the first time. Zero construction experience,
doesn't know what's normal (is a 2-week delay serious or trivial?), and
this person's anxiety is exactly what motivated the product (see
[01-overview.md](../../01-overview.md)).

## Motivations

To feel they have control and visibility over something they don't
technically master. They don't want to learn how to manage a build —
they want to trust that someone is doing that well, with proof.

## Pain points

- Doesn't know how to interpret technical information even when they have
  it — is an 8% budget deviation bad or normal? They can't judge alone.
- Anxiety leads to checking the app more often than would be necessary,
  looking for reassurance, not data.
- Fear of seeming "annoying" by questioning the contractor, but also fear
  of being deceived by not questioning.

## Behaviors

- Mobile phone, frequent but brief use — several times a week, short
  sessions.
- Responds more to simple language and visual signals (green/yellow/red)
  than to raw numbers.

## Where this differs from other variants of the same role

Unlike the
[experienced owner](./02-experienced-owner-investor.md), they have no
reference point to calibrate whether something is concerning — they need
the app to do that interpretation for them, not just show the data.

## Implications for features

- **In favor of**: badges/indicators in simple language ("on track" /
  "attention" / "delayed") over raw data — without hiding the detail, but
  without forcing them to interpret it alone.
- **In favor of**: notifications that proactively reassure, not just alert
  to problems — "no changes this week" has value for this variant, even
  as a non-news item.
- **Against**: dashboards dense with numbers without context — these work
  for the [experienced owner](./02-experienced-owner-investor.md), not for
  this variant.
